The opportunity

Screen time is part of family life.

Children use connected devices for entertainment, learning and discovery. Parents and educators want practical ways to set boundaries without turning every screen-time decision into an argument.

How it works

A short learning moment
between viewing sessions.

Owlyay is designed to manage a child's permitted screen time and offer a brief learning activity at parent- or school-defined intervals.

  1. Child starts an approved appOne the parent has chosen to allow
    1
  2. Viewing allowance is trackedTime counts while the app is on screen
    2
  3. Learning break is presentedA short, age-appropriate challenge
    3
  4. Parent-defined access resumesA new allowance, within the daily limit
    4

The loop repeats until the daily limit is reached

An illustrative rule

Watch, learn, carry on.

  1. 10 minutesof permitted viewing
  2. 1 short challengemaths, matched to the child's level
  3. A new allowanceonce the challenge is complete

Parents can configure the interval, the activity and the daily limit.

How the break interrupts depends on the device: on iPhone and iPad, iOS itself covers the app; on Android, Owlyay brings its question to the front.

For schools

Structured device routines.

A potential tool for schools using managed tablets, such as school iPads or Android tablets, supporting classroom routines, short practice breaks and consistent device‑use policies.

Classroom routines

Set teacher-led schedules and short activity intervals on supported, school‑managed devices.

Skill practice

Assign age- or level-appropriate maths, vocabulary, memory and general‑knowledge activities.

Group insights

Review aggregate participation and learning trends, with access controls and student privacy safeguards.

School deployment: school-wide enforcement needs compatible managed devices and administrator-approved provisioning. We will never claim Owlyay can control every personally owned device just by installing an app.

Honest about protection

Three set-ups,
three real levels of protection.

Every device shows its real level. Owlyay never pretends an app is locked.

Android phones & tablets

Standard mode

A normal app install, with permissions a parent grants during setup.

In the pilot app
  • Counts the time while a watched app is on screen
  • Brings the learning break to the front when time is up

Honest limit: a child can switch permissions off. Owlyay tells you, but cannot prevent it.

iPhone & iPad

Screen Time mode

Apple's Screen Time permission, approved with a parent's Apple Account.

In the pilot app
  • iOS itself covers the chosen apps when time is up
  • With a child Apple Account, only a parent can switch it off

Honest limit: iOS can report time late. Without a child account, a child could switch it off.

School-managed devices

Managed mode

A reset device enrolled by a school or device administrator.

Planned
  • The operating system itself pauses the app until the break is done
  • Could run through a school's device management

Honest limit: needs a reset device and an administrator, so it is not for family phones.

Trust & responsible use

Built on trust, compatibility
and responsible use.

Honest device support

Phones, tablets and TVs differ by maker, OS version and management capability. We will publish a tested compatibility list for iPhone, iPad and Android, and explain every permission we ask for.

Respect platform rules

A parental-control layer, not a modified YouTube player: Owlyay never alters playback, removes ads or embeds YouTube. On iPhone and iPad it works only through Apple's Screen Time.

Child privacy

The pilot apps keep everything on the device. Any future service collects only what family controls and learning need, with parent consent, data minimisation and retention controls. School deployments get additional review.

Healthy learning experience

Supportive feedback, sensible break lengths and parent-configurable rules. No shame, punitive messages, excessive testing or incentives to spend more time on screens.

This proposal describes a product concept, not a claim that every listed enforcement capability already works on every device. Technical feasibility, platform terms, device‑level restrictions, privacy obligations and school procurement requirements must be validated before launch.
