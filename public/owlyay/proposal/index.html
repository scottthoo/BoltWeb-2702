The opportunity

Screen time is part of family life.
Let's make it more intentional.

Children use connected devices for entertainment, learning and discovery. Parents and educators want practical ways to set boundaries without turning every screen-time decision into an argument.

How it works

A short learning moment
between viewing sessions.

Owlyay is designed to manage a child's permitted screen time and offer a brief learning activity at parent- or school-defined intervals.

  1. Child starts an approved appOne the parent has chosen to allow
    1
  2. Viewing allowance is trackedTime counts while the app is on screen
    2
  3. Learning break is presentedA short, age-appropriate challenge
    3
  4. Parent-defined access resumesA new allowance, within the daily limit
    4

The loop repeats until the daily limit is reached

An illustrative rule

Watch, learn, carry on.

  1. 10 minutesof permitted viewing
  2. 1 short challengemaths, matched to the child's level
  3. A new allowanceonce the challenge is complete

Parents can configure the interval, the activity and the daily limit.

The exact interruption and enforcement behaviour depends on each Android device's capabilities and permissions.

For schools

Structured device routines.

A potential tool for schools using managed Android devices or tablets, supporting classroom routines, short practice breaks and consistent device-use policies.

Classroom routines

Set teacher-led schedules and short activity intervals on supported, school-managed devices.

Skill practice

Assign age- or level-appropriate maths, vocabulary, memory and general-knowledge activities.

Group insights

Review aggregate participation and learning trends, with access controls and student privacy safeguards.

School deployment: school-wide enforcement needs compatible managed devices and administrator-approved provisioning. We will never claim Owlyay can control every personally owned device just by installing an app.

Product experience

Friendly for children.
Practical for adults.

Children see short, encouraging challenges. Parents and educators get a calm, practical place to set the rules and follow progress.

Child experience

Simple, encouraging challenges

Honest about protection

Two set-ups,
two real levels of protection.

Every device shows its true protection level. Owlyay never pretends an app is locked when it is not.

Family phones & tablets

Standard mode

A normal app install, using permissions a parent grants during setup.

Designed to
  • Notice when a watched app is on screen and count the time
  • Bring the learning break to the front when time is up
  • Tell the parent if a permission is switched off

Honest limit: a determined child can switch permissions off in Settings. Owlyay can tell you, but cannot prevent it.

School-managed devices

Managed mode

A reset device enrolled by a school or device administrator.

Designed to
  • Have the operating system itself pause the app until the break is done
  • Be much harder for a child to get around
  • Possibly run through a school's existing device-management system

Honest limit: needs a reset device and administrator setup, so it is not a simple app install for families.

Android TV and Google TV are a separate track, supported only after testing on real hardware.

Trust & responsible use

Built on trust, compatibility
and responsible use.

Honest device support

Android TV, Google TV and phones differ by manufacturer, OS version and management capability. We will publish a tested compatibility list and explain every permission we ask for.

Respect platform rules

A parental-control layer, not a modified YouTube player. Owlyay will not alter playback, remove ads, intercept player controls or embed YouTube in a custom player.

Child privacy

Collect only what family controls and learning need. Parent consent, data minimisation, retention controls and age-appropriate safeguards. School deployments get additional review.

Healthy learning experience

Supportive feedback, sensible break lengths and parent-configurable rules. No shame, punitive messages, excessive testing or incentives to spend more time on screens.

This proposal describes a product concept, not a claim that every listed enforcement capability already works on every Android device. Technical feasibility, platform terms, device‑level restrictions, privacy obligations and school procurement requirements must be validated before launch.
